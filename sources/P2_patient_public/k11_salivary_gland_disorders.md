## Verified takeaways

- Saliva helps keep the mouth moist and supports healthy teeth, and the major salivary glands are the parotid, sublingual, and submandibular glands.
- Salivary gland blockage or stones can cause swelling and pain, especially when saliva flow increases during meals.
- Common symptoms of blockage can include dull intermittent pain, swelling that flares up, and infection signs such as pain, redness, or fever.
- Conservative self-care for stones includes drinking more water, gentle massage, and sugar-free gum or sweets to stimulate saliva flow.
