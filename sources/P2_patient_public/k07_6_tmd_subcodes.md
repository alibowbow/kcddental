## Verified takeaways

- TMDs are a group of disorders affecting the jaw joint and the muscles that control jaw movement.
- Common symptoms include jaw pain, stiffness, limited opening, locking, and clicking or popping; clicking without pain can be normal.
- TMDs often improve with conservative care, and there is no single standard test for diagnosis.
- The public sources separate joint problems, disc/internal derangement, muscle pain, noise, stiffness, and arthritis into different patient-friendly patterns.

## Scope note

- Keep this note limited to TMJ and chewing-muscle disorders.
- Do not use it for jaw-size anomalies, tooth-position problems, or general malocclusion unless the clinician clearly calls it a TMD.
- If the note only says jaw pain without a TMD diagnosis, do not jump straight to K07.6.

## Safe wording

- `K07.60`: use when the record names disc displacement, internal derangement, or a slipped/displaced TMJ disc.
- `K07.61`: use when the main finding is clicking, popping, cracking, or other joint noise.
- `K07.62`: use when the TMJ repeatedly dislocates or subluxates.
- `K07.63`: use when the chart specifically documents TMJ pain or arthralgia.
- `K07.64`: use when the chart specifically documents TMJ stiffness, locking, or limited opening.
- `K07.65`: use when degenerative change or arthritis of the TMJ is documented.
- `K07.66`: use when the problem is in the chewing muscles or myofascial pain rather than the joint itself.
- `K07.68`: use when a specific TMJ disorder is documented but the name does not match the named subcodes.
- `K07.69`: use when the record says TMJ disorder but gives no subtype detail.

## Limits / ambiguities

- Clicking without pain can be normal, so do not code `K07.61` from a click alone unless the clinician treats it as a disorder.
- Joint, muscle, and pain categories overlap often; use the diagnosis the clinician actually documented.
- Conservative care is common, so procedure notes alone are not enough to assign a subtype without a clear diagnosis.
