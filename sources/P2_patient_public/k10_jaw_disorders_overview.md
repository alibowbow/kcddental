## Verified takeaways

- K10 can be summarized safely only at a high level because it mixes developmental jaw defects, inflammatory jawbone problems, alveolitis, and osteonecrosis.
- Shared patient-facing warning signs are jaw pain, swelling, chewing trouble, delayed healing after extraction, and the need for imaging or specialist review.
- Recent extraction history and medication history are especially important when jaw symptoms do not settle as expected.
- Root-level treatment language should stay broad: supportive care for some subtypes, specialist procedures for others.

## Scope note

- This note is an umbrella overview, not a substitute for subtype pages.
- Keep `etiology`, `treatment`, and `prognosis` phrasing conservative because K10 is clinically heterogeneous.
