## Verified takeaways

- K00.6 is a broad eruption bucket, but the safest patient-facing way to describe it is as problems with when teeth appear, whether they appear, and whether baby teeth are retained longer than expected.
- MedlinePlus supports delayed or absent tooth formation, normal timing variation, dental X-rays when needed, and evaluation when no teeth have appeared by 9 months.
- Natal teeth are teeth present at birth; they can be loose, poorly formed, irritate the tongue, and are sometimes removed if there is aspiration risk.
- UCLH’s hypodontia clinic materials support a conservative view of retained primary teeth, hidden adult teeth that do not erupt, and the need for orthodontic or surgical planning when eruption is blocked or abnormal.

## Scope note

- This is a root-and-subcode navigation note, not a full dental development textbook.
- It is safe to use for `K00.60` through `K00.69`, but each child code should still be mapped conservatively:
- `K00.60` and `K00.61` can be anchored to congenital or natal teeth only.
- `K00.62` and `K00.64` should be kept to early or late eruption timing, not every developmental anomaly.
- `K00.63` should be reserved for retained baby teeth when the permanent successor is missing or delayed.
- `K00.65` should be used only when a primary tooth is lost earlier than expected.
- `K00.68` and `K00.69` should stay as fallback buckets for unclear cases.
