# K00.0 patient-public note: Delayed or absent tooth formation

- target code: `K00.0`
- source title: `Tooth formation - delayed or absent | MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia`
- source url: `https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003061.htm`
- review date: `2024-03-31`
- additional source title: `Teething: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia`
- additional source url: `https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002045.htm`
- additional review date: `2024-10-20`

## Verified takeaways

- K00.0 is best understood as delayed or absent tooth formation, including teeth that are missing because they never developed.
- MedlinePlus lists alternative names that include hypodontia, oligodontia, and anodontia, which makes this page useful for the K00.0 family rather than only complete anodontia.
- A normal first tooth often appears between 4 and 8 months, but timing varies, so the absence of teeth is assessed by age and context.
- If a child has no teeth by 9 months, MedlinePlus advises contacting a health professional.
- Dental X-rays and family history help separate simple timing variation from a developmental condition.
- Missing-teeth cases can sometimes be corrected with cosmetic or orthodontic dentistry.

## Subcode mapping

- `K00.00` fits best when only part of the tooth set never develops.
- `K00.01` fits best when there is complete absence of teeth.
- `K00.09` is safest when the record says delayed or absent formation but does not specify the extent.

## Scope note

- This note is a conservative umbrella for congenital missing-tooth and delayed-formation patterns.
- Do not use it for teeth that are present but trapped or blocked from erupting; that belongs under `K01`.
- Do not use it for tooth shape or structure abnormalities; those are better handled by other `K00` subgroups.
