## Verified takeaways

- K07.0 is best treated as a jaw-size and jaw-proportion bucket, not as one disease.
- Micrognathia is a lower jaw that is smaller than normal, and it can interfere with feeding, leave not enough room for the teeth, and affect alignment.
- Malocclusion can happen when the upper and lower jaws differ in size or when jaw size does not match tooth size, and class 2 and class 3 bites are common patient-facing examples of that mismatch.
- Orthognathic surgery is used when orthodontics alone cannot correct jaw alignment or size imbalance, and it can improve chewing, facial balance, lip closure, and bite fit.
- Jaw surgery may involve maxillary, mandibular, or bimaxillary osteotomy, often with braces before and after surgery.

## Scope note

- Keep this note at the level of jaw size discrepancy and jaw proportion mismatch.
- Do not use it as a general malocclusion note, because tooth-position and TMJ problems belong in other K07 subgroups.
- Do not infer macrognathia or micrognathia from bite appearance alone unless the record explicitly states a jaw-size problem.

## Conservative child-code guidance

- Use `K07.00` when the upper jaw is explicitly documented as enlarged or prominent.
- Use `K07.01` when the lower jaw is explicitly documented as enlarged or prominent.
- Use `K07.02` when both jaws are explicitly described as enlarged or disproportionate.
- Use `K07.03` when the upper jaw is explicitly documented as small or underdeveloped.
- Use `K07.04` when the lower jaw is explicitly documented as small or underdeveloped.
- Use `K07.05` when both jaws are explicitly documented as small or underdeveloped.
- Use `K07.08` only when a jaw-size anomaly is clearly documented but does not fit the named patterns above.
- Use `K07.09` when the record says there is a jaw-size abnormality but the exact pattern is not described.
- Do not code `K07.0` from tooth crowding, mouth breathing, or bite problems alone unless the source also says the jaw itself is too large, too small, or otherwise disproportionate.
