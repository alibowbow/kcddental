## Verified takeaways

- K00.3 is most safely explained as mottling or abnormal discoloration of enamel, with dental fluorosis as the common patient-facing example.
- CDC notes that too much fluoride while teeth are developing can produce white flecks, spots, or lines in the enamel.
- Mild fluorosis is usually cosmetic and not painful, while more obvious cases can show broader enamel changes.
- MedlinePlus adds that tooth discoloration can also come from genetics, infection, medications, fever during tooth formation, or other enamel defects, so mottling should not be assumed to be fluoride alone.

## Scope note

- Keep this note narrow and patient-facing.
- Use fluorosis as the safest anchor, but do not equate every mottled tooth with fluorosis.
- More specific child codes should only be used when the pattern is clearly documented.

## Conservative child-code guidance

- Use `K00.30` when mottling is consistent with fluorosis or fluoride-associated enamel spotting.
- Use `K00.31` when the discoloration is non-fluorotic or the source specifically suggests another enamel opacity pattern.
- Use `K00.39` when the record says mottled or abnormal color but does not specify the subtype.
