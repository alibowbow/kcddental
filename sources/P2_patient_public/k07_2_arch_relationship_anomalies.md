## Verified takeaways

- K07.2 is best treated as an arch-relationship and bite-pattern bucket, not as a tooth-shape, jaw-size, or TMJ bucket.
- MedlinePlus says malocclusion is improper alignment of the teeth, and it can happen because the upper and lower jaws do not align the way they should.
- Cleveland Clinic and the AAO both describe the main bite patterns that sit inside this family: overjet, overbite/deep bite, open bite, crossbite, underbite, crowding, and spacing-related shifts.
- NHS orthodontics guidance supports that these problems are commonly managed with braces, headgear, or sometimes surgery when the bite and jaw relationship cannot be corrected with braces alone.
- Royal Devon notes that orthognathic surgery is used when there is an upper/lower jaw imbalance that orthodontics alone cannot fix.
- Because the official subcodes mix jaw-position language and bite-language, the safest coding rule is to code only what is clearly documented in the record.

## Scope note

- Keep this note focused on how the upper and lower dental arches meet.
- Do not use it for jaw-size imbalance alone; that belongs under `K07.0`.
- Do not use it for tooth-position anomalies alone; those belong under `K07.3`.
- Do not use it for tongue habits, mouth breathing, or swallowing patterns unless the bite pattern itself is documented.
- Do not use it for TMJ pain or joint clicking alone; that belongs under `K07.6`.

## Conservative child-code guidance

- Use `K07.20` when the chart clearly says the lower arch or lower front teeth sit behind the upper arch in a class II / disto-occlusion pattern, or when an overjet-type bite is described as the main arch relationship.
- Use `K07.21` when the chart clearly says the lower arch or lower front teeth sit ahead of the upper arch in a class III / mesio-occlusion pattern, or when an underbite / reverse overjet is described as the main arch relationship.
- Use `K07.22` when the record specifically describes excessive horizontal projection of the upper front teeth, not just a general class II bite.
- Use `K07.23` when the record specifically describes a deep vertical overlap of the upper front teeth over the lower front teeth, not just any overbite wording.
- Use `K07.24` when the teeth do not touch in the front and/or back while the mouth is closed, especially if the chart names open bite.
- Use `K07.25` when the upper teeth fit inside the lower teeth in the front and/or back, including anterior or posterior crossbite.
- Use `K07.26` when the dental midlines are shifted and the upper and lower arches do not line up in the center.
- Use `K07.27` only when the record explicitly describes the official posterior lingual occlusion pattern of the mandibular posterior teeth; avoid guessing this from general crowding or midline shift.
- Use `K07.28` when an arch-relationship anomaly is clearly present but the record gives enough detail to exclude the named patterns above.
- Use `K07.29` when the record says there is an arch-relationship anomaly or malocclusion but does not state which subtype it is.

## Limits / ambiguities

- Class II and Class III wording can describe either the teeth or the jaws, so do not upgrade a tooth-only note to a jaw-growth diagnosis unless the source says the jaws themselves are positioned that way.
- Overjet, overbite, underbite, and crossbite often overlap in real patients, so the best code is the one that the record emphasizes most clearly.
- Midline deviation is often cosmetic and functional rather than painful, so code it only when it is explicitly documented as an anomaly.
- The K07.27 label is unusually specific and is easiest to misuse, so keep it conservative.
