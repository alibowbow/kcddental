## Verified takeaways

- K07.1 is best treated as a skeletal jaw-position category, not a simple tooth-crowding category.
- The common patient-facing signs are facial asymmetry, an upper/lower jaw size mismatch, a prominent or recessed jaw/chin, and a bite that does not come together normally.
- MedlinePlus supports the link between jaw size differences and malocclusion, including Class 2 overbite/retrognathism and Class 3 underbite/prognathism.
- NHS hospital pages describe orthognathic surgery as repositioning the jaws to improve bite, chewing, facial balance, lip closure, and jaw alignment.
- Craniofacial microsomia is a useful conservative example for jaw asymmetry because it can affect one or both sides of the face, including the jaws and teeth.
- Use specialist assessment, not appearance alone, to separate a skeletal jaw relationship problem from a primarily dental bite problem.

## Scope note

- Use this note for K07.1 orientation and child-code mapping only.
- Keep the language patient-friendly and avoid implying a diagnosis from one symptom.
- Prefer skeletal jaw relationship language over tooth-only language when the record clearly describes jaw-base mismatch.

## Conservative child-code guidance

- `K07.10`: use when the record clearly says the jaw or face is asymmetric, with one side smaller, shifted, or uneven.
- `K07.11`: use when the lower jaw is prominent or forward, including patient-facing terms like underbite or prognathism.
- `K07.12`: use when the upper jaw is prominent or forward, especially when the note clearly distinguishes upper-jaw prominence from general malocclusion.
- `K07.13`: use when the lower jaw is recessed or backward, including retruded chin or retrognathism wording.
- `K07.14`: use when the upper jaw is recessed or backward, including maxillary retrusion or midface retrusion wording.
- `K07.18`: use for a documented skeletal jaw-base mismatch that does not fit the named patterns above, such as another specified jaw asymmetry or proportion problem.
- `K07.19`: use only when the record says there is a jaw-cranial base relationship anomaly but does not give enough detail to name the subtype.

## Limits and ambiguities

- In everyday language, `K07.1` can overlap with malocclusion, overbite, underbite, or orthodontic problems, so the chart context matters.
- Facial asymmetry alone is not enough to prove a skeletal jaw-base anomaly without dental or specialist confirmation.
- The children under `K07.1` are clinical mapping aids; real notes may use different wording such as "jaw imbalance", "recessive chin", or "prominent jaw".
- If the problem is mostly tooth position without a skeletal jaw mismatch, `K07.2` or `K07.3` may be a better fit than `K07.1`.
