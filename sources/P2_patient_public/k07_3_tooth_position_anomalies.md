## Verified takeaways

- K07.3 is a tooth-position bucket, not a jaw-size or jaw-joint bucket.
- Malocclusion can come from crowding, missing teeth, extra teeth, impacted teeth, thumb sucking, tongue thrusting, or a mismatch between jaw size and tooth size.
- Crowding means there is not enough room for the teeth, so they can overlap, twist, or be pushed forward or backward.
- Spacing means there are gaps between teeth, and it can happen with missing teeth, undersized teeth, or a wide jaw.
- Crossbite, open bite, and underbite are bite-pattern problems that may overlap with other K07 groups, so keep this note focused on tooth position itself.
- Impacted teeth are teeth that fail to erupt through the gum line, often because of lack of space or an abnormal eruption angle, and they can disturb neighboring tooth position.
- Rotated teeth are teeth turned around their long axis; use that wording only when the source explicitly says the tooth is rotated or turned.

## Scope note

- Keep this note at the level of where individual teeth sit, point, or erupt in the arch.
- Do not use it for jaw-size mismatch, jaw relationship problems, or temporomandibular disorders.
- If the record only says "bad bite" without describing the tooth position pattern, use `K07.4` instead.

## Conservative child-code guidance

- Use `K07.30` for crowding, overlapping teeth, or blocked-out teeth.
- Use `K07.31` for a tooth or teeth displaced from the normal arch line.
- Use `K07.32` for a rotated or turned tooth.
- Use `K07.33` for spacing, gaps, or diastema.
- Use `K07.34` only if your mapping keeps the official duplicate displacement label distinct from `K07.31`; clinically it means the same displaced-tooth idea.
- Use `K07.35` for impacted or unerupted tooth with a positional abnormality.
- Use `K07.38` for another named tooth-position anomaly that does not fit the codes above.
- Use `K07.39` when the record says there is a tooth-position anomaly but the exact pattern is not described.

## Limits and ambiguities

- `K07.31` and `K07.34` are both labeled as displacement in the official hierarchy, so they are a code-structure oddity rather than two clearly different clinical concepts.
- Do not infer rotation, spacing, or impaction from crowding alone.
- Do not move jaw relationship problems into this bucket just because the bite looks abnormal.
